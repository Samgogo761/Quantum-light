Electrically Activating and Switching the Magneto-Optic Faraday
Effect in 2D Antiferromagnets
Liyuan Zhang 1,*, Chen Liang 1
 and Chuanhui Gong 2,*

Abstract
Two-dimensional (2D) antiferromagnets are highly promising for next-generation spin-
tronics due to their ultrafast dynamics and robustness against stray fields; however, their
practical application is severely hindered by the vanishing magneto-optic effects restricted
by strict crystal symmetries. In this work, we propose a universal physical mechanism to
activate and manipulate the magneto-optic Faraday effect in 2D fully compensated bilayer
antiferromagnets using an external vertical electric field. By constructing a comprehen-
sive tight-binding model and performing first-principles calculations on bilayer VSe2, we
demonstrate that the applied electric field explicitly breaks the spatial inversion and com-
bined P T symmetries. This symmetry breaking lifts the Kramers degeneracy, inducing a
pronounced spin splitting that, in conjunction with intrinsic spin–orbit coupling, generates
non-vanishing Berry curvature. Consequently, the previously forbidden Faraday rotation
angle is activated from zero to a significant non-zero value, and its rotation direction can
be deterministically reversed by switching the electric field polarity. Our findings provide
profound physical insights into the symmetry-modulated light–matter interactions and
pave the way for designing fully electrically controllable, energy-efficient antiferromagnetic
opto-spintronic devices.
Keywords: 2D antiferromagnets; magneto-optic Faraday effect; first-principles calculations
1. Introduction
In the exploration of next-generation high-density and ultrafast spintronic devices,
antiferromagnets and fully compensated magnets are gradually replacing traditional fer-
romagnets as a central focus in condensed matter physics and materials science [ 1–8].
Compared to ferromagnets, antiferromagnetic systems feature zero macroscopic net mag-
netization, which fundamentally avoids bit crosstalk caused by stray magnetic fields,
enabling extremely high-density information storage [ 9–13]. Meanwhile, they exhibit
strong robustness against external magnetic field perturbations, and their spin dynamic
responses typically lie in the terahertz (THz) frequency range, which is three orders of
magnitude faster than that of ferromagnets [14–16]. In recent years, with the vigorous de-
velopment of two-dimensional (2D) magnetic systems, researchers have made substantial
breakthroughs in the precise synthesis, magnetic order manipulation, and electrical readout
of 2D antiferromagnetic materials [17–21]. These advancements have not only profoundly
broadened our physical understanding of low-dimensional magnetism but also laid a

solid foundation for the development of smaller, faster, and more stable antiferromagnetic
spintronic devices [22–26].
Despite the tremendous potential of antiferromagnets in electrical transport and dynam-
ics, achieving non-contact, high-resolution detection and optoelectronic applications of their
magnetic states remains a major challenge [27–31]. In this context, magneto-optic effects (such
as the magneto-optic Faraday effect), serving as a bridge between magnetism and optics, play
an indispensable role in opto-spintronic devices and information reading/writing [32–35].
However, for antiferromagnets and fully compensated magnets, their conventional linear
magneto-optic effects are often forbidden by severe physical restrictions. This prohibition
stems from specific crystal symmetries universally present in their lattice structures, such as
the combined spatial inversion and time-reversal symmetry (PT symmetry), or the combined
two-fold rotation and time-reversal symmetry (C2T symmetry) [36–38]. According to the
symmetry principles of physics, these symmetry operations force the system’s responses to for-
ward and backward propagating light waves to cancel out exactly , resulting in a macroscopic
magneto-optic Faraday rotation angle that is strictly zero [39,40]. Therefore, effectively break-
ing these inherent symmetry restrictions to activate and manipulate the magneto-optic effect
in fully compensated antiferromagnetic systems has become a critical obstacle to overcome in
the current field of antiferromagnetic optoelectronics.
In this work, we reveal a universal physical mechanism for manipulating the magneto-
optic response of 2D antiferromagnetic materials using an external electric field, and
conduct an in-depth study using bilayer VSe2 as a representative material (which exhibits
ferromagnetism in the monolayer limit but becomes antiferromagnetic in the bilayer form
due to interlayer coupling) [41–43]. Our results demonstrate that without an applied electric
field, the two layers of bilayer VSe2 are strictly constrained by crystal symmetries such as
P T and C2T , rendering its magneto-optic Faraday effect completely silent (i.e., the Faraday
angle is zero). However, upon introducing an external electric field perpendicular to the
2D plane, the electric field directly and effectively breaks the spatial symmetry connection
between the layers. This symmetry breaking disrupts the original optical response balance,
successfully realizing the activation of the magneto-optic Faraday effect from zero to
a significant non-zero Faraday rotation angle. More importantly, we demonstrate that
simply switching the polarity (positive/negative direction) of the perpendicular electric
field can directly reverse the direction of the Faraday rotation angle. Our study not only
provides profound physical insights into the microscopic mechanism of symmetry and
light–matter interactions in 2D antiferromagnetic systems but also offers a practical and
universal approach for designing fully electrically controlled magneto-optic switches and
next-generation antiferromagnetic optoelectronic information processing devices based on
compensated magnets.

set to U = 2.7 eV . Symmetry-adapted Wannier functions were generated using VASP in

2.2. Symmetry Analysis and Theoretical Foundation for Electric-Field-Induced Magneto-Optic Effects
In exploring the physical origin of magneto-optic effects in two-dimensional (2D)
bilayer antiferromagnetic systems, crystal symmetry plays a decisive role. According to
Neumann’s principle, any macroscopic physical property of a material must be invariant
under all symmetry operations of its point group. For an ideal 2D bilayer fully compensated
antiferromagnet (e.g., bilayer VSe2), in the absence of external perturbations, the upper
and lower layers possess magnetic moments of equal magnitude but opposite directions.
In this pristine state, although the system as a whole breaks time-reversal symmetry (T ),
it is often protected by a joint symmetry due to strict geometric symmetry operations
connecting the two layers, such as in-plane two-fold rotation ( C2), spatial inversion (P),
or interlayer mirror symmetry (M). For instance, the spatial inversion P maps the spin
sites of the upper layer to the lower layer, while time-reversal T flips the antiparallel spins.
Therefore, the entire bilayer system remains invariant under the combined operationP T (or
similarly, C2T , MT ). It is precisely this strict symmetry that forces the system’s responses
to forward- and backward-propagating light to cancel out exactly, thereby strictly restricting
the macroscopic observables of the anomalous Hall effect (AHE) and the magneto-optic
Faraday effect to zero.
To activate these forbidden physical effects in such systems, an external field must
be introduced to break the spatial symmetries connecting the upper and lower layers.
Applying an external gate electric field (Ez) perpendicular to the 2D plane provides a highly
precise and efficient tuning method. A vertical electric field is inherently a polar vector,
which changes sign (i.e., reverses direction) under spatial inversion P and in-plane rotation
C2 operations. Consequently, when an external field Ez ̸= 0 is applied, it introduces an
electrostatic potential difference between the upper and lower layers (i.e., the Stark effect).
This interlayer electrostatic asymmetry directly and completely breaks all lattice symmetry
operations that can map the upper layer to the lower layer (including P, C2, and M).
The complete breaking of symmetry triggers a profound physical phase transition.
Since the upper and lower layers are no longer physically equivalent under the electric field,

With the destruction of spatial lattice symmetries (P, C2, M), the original combined

only achieve a magnetic phase transition from an antiferromagnet to a fully compensated
ferrimagnet but also theoretically establish a solid foundation for the electrically controllable
“zero to non-zero” magneto-optic Faraday effect.

2.4. Material Realization in Bilayer VSe2
To substantiate the physical insights derived from the tight-binding model and verify
the universality of our theory in realistic materials, we performed first-principles calcula-
tions based on density functional theory (DFT) on bilayer VSe2. While monolayer VSe2
exhibits intrinsic ferromagnetism, its bilayer counterpart favors an interlayer fully compen-
sated antiferromagnetic ground state driven by the interlayer antiferromagnetic exchange
coupling, as illustrated in Figure 4a. Symmetry analysis reveals that the antiferromagnetic
bilayer VSe2 belongs to the magnetic space group P¯3′m′1 (No. 164.88). Crucially, this spe-
cific structure strictly preserves the combined spatial inversion and time-reversal symmetry
(P T ). Under this symmetry operation, the magnetic vanadium (V) atoms located in the top
and bottom layers are geometrically mapped onto each other, possessing local magnetic
moments of equal magnitude but strictly opposite directions. This rigorous crystallographic
and magnetic symmetry leaves a distinct signature on the electronic band structure. As
shown in Figure 4b, the DFT-calculated band structure in the absence of an applied electric
field exhibits perfect degeneracy between the spin-up and spin-down bands across the
entire Brillouin zone, which is a hallmark of P T -symmetric compensated antiferromagnets.

3. Conclusions
In summary, we have theoretically and computationally demonstrated the electric-
field-induced activation and reversible tuning of the magneto-optic Faraday effect in 2D
fully compensated bilayer antiferromagnets. Using a symmetry-based tight-binding model,
we revealed that a vertical gate electric field breaks the inherent spatial inversion (P) and
joint P T symmetries, triggering a phase transition from an antiferromagnetic state to a
fully compensated ferrimagnetic state. The interplay between the electric-field-induced
spin splitting and intrinsic spin–orbit coupling generates non-zero Berry curvature, which
subsequently activates the macroscopic Faraday effect. These theoretical predictions are
rigorously corroborated by first-principles calculations on bilayer VSe2. We show that the
magnitude of the Faraday rotation angle increases monotonically with the electric field
strength, and its sign can be perfectly switched by reversing the field direction. This work
not only broadens our fundamental understanding of symmetry-broken optical responses
in low-dimensional magnetism but also establishes a practical paradigm for developing
high-density, ultrafast, and fully electrically tunable magneto-optic modulators based on
2D compensated magnets.
